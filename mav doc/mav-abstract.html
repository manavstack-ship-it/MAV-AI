MAV
An AI assistant built from a plant's own knowledge — running on its own infrastructure.

Your data, your model, your assistant

MAV turns a company's existing industrial knowledge — manuals, machine documentation, maintenance records, reports, and the expertise living in people's heads — into a private AI assistant employees can simply ask. Unlike a generic chatbot layered on top of company files, MAV is trained on that company's own operational reality and runs inside its own infrastructure, so proprietary data never has to leave the building. That combination — company-specific knowledge plus on-premises AI — is what makes MAV different from both generic AI tools and traditional knowledge-management software.

The problem

Knowledge is trapped, scattered, and hard to reach

Industrial knowledge lives in SOPs, machine docs, maintenance logs, operational reports, and experienced employees — spread across different systems no single tool searches.

  • Finding the right procedure takes time employees don't have
  • Experienced staff become single points of knowledge
  • New hires need long, costly onboarding
  • Generic AI doesn't understand the company's own processes
  • Sending sensitive plant data to external AI is a non-starter for most operators

The solution

Data in, an assistant out

MAV connects to a company's existing documents and systems, indexes them, and serves them back through a natural-language assistant employees already know how to use.

  • One assistant that speaks the plant's own vocabulary
  • Answers cite the company's own procedures, not generic advice
  • Cuts search time and reduces reliance on any one expert
  • Deployed inside the company's infrastructure, not a public cloud
  • Remembers conversation and session context across a shift
How Qwen AI is integrated
A self-hosted reasoning layer, not a cloud API call

MAV's reasoning is powered by Qwen AI, served locally through Ollama instead of an external API. A FastAPI backend retrieves the relevant company documents and conversation memory for a query, optionally enriches it with live web results via Tavily, assembles the company-specific context, and passes it to Qwen AI running inside the customer's own network — so the model doing the reasoning never has to send data outside the plant to work.

Company Data→ FastAPI→ MAV Intelligence Layer→ Qwen AI (via Ollama)→ Response

Runs on-premises

Qwen's open weights let the whole model be self-hosted, so inference happens on hardware the company controls.

Sized to fit

Strong reasoning and multilingual performance at sizes that can be quantized to a customer's own GPU budget.

No data egress

No per-token calls to an external provider, so the privacy concern named above is solved by design, not policy.
